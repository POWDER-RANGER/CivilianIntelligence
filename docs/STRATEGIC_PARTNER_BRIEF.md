# CIVINTELLIGENCE — Strategic Partner Brief

**Purpose:** Product-fit, licensing, investment, and acquisition discussions  
**Public web application:** https://civintelligence.onrender.com  
**Primary repository:** https://github.com/POWDER-RANGER/CivilianIntelligence

## Overview

CIVINTELLIGENCE is an evidence-first civic research platform designed to help users discover public sources, follow institutional activity, connect records, and understand public infrastructure in context. Its product approach emphasizes source traceability, explicit uncertainty, and a distinction between source records and platform-authored analysis.

The project is intended to make public records easier to find and inspect—not to target private individuals.

## Product surfaces

The repository and public application organize the work into several related workflows:

- **Source discovery:** a catalog and research starting points for civic and government sources.
- **Public-record workflows:** FOIA and related request-generation tools.
- **Finance and oversight:** public spending, filings, accountability, and institutional records.
- **Geospatial context:** a MapLibre-based infrastructure/oversight map with public-source provenance.
- **Record evidence model:** source-native identifiers, retrieval metadata, hashes, version history, and typed relationships are part of the documented Record Index design.
- **Integration architecture:** contracts connect the main application to specialized CIVWATCH components and public-data ingestion.

Availability varies by workflow and upstream source. A public deployment does not, by itself, mean every source adapter or integration has passed production acceptance.

## Potential strategic fit

CIVINTELLIGENCE may be relevant to organizations building:

- Civic technology and public accountability tools.
- Investigative research and public-record discovery products.
- Government transparency, compliance, and policy-research workflows.
- Geospatial research and public-infrastructure context tools.
- Document, data integration, or evidence-management products.

The best partnership is one with a specific user problem and an existing distribution channel, dataset, or workflow that can make the platform more useful.

## What is ready for evaluation

- Public web application and source repository.
- Architecture and cross-repository integration documentation.
- Existing TypeScript/React application and public-data ingestion code.
- Documented provenance and evidence-handling principles.
- A concrete roadmap for production acceptance and commercialization.

## Current limitations to disclose

- No revenue or paying customers are currently reported.
- Production acceptance of all core workflows has not yet been demonstrated in a single current test report.
- Some data feeds depend on external providers and may be rate-limited, unavailable, or stale.
- The repository declares an MIT license. A prospective partner must evaluate the actual license and cannot assume exclusive rights to publicly available code.
- Third-party data, dependencies, domains, hosted services, marks, and separately operated components require an asset-by-asset rights review.
- Customer demand and willingness to pay remain to be validated.

## Partnership options

1. **Paid pilot:** test one defined workflow with written acceptance criteria and a short evaluation period.
2. **Commercial integration:** connect selected CIVINTELLIGENCE capabilities to an existing product.
3. **Licensing and support:** define the scope of support, implementation, maintenance, and any rights beyond the public MIT-licensed code.
4. **Strategic investment or acquisition:** evaluate the codebase, documented architecture, transferable assets, production evidence, and product roadmap through a structured diligence process.

Commercial terms should be negotiated based on scope, rights, responsibilities, and demonstrated buyer value. No particular valuation or transaction is represented as agreed or guaranteed.

## Proposed first conversation

A useful initial discussion should answer:

1. Which user workflow would the partner want to improve?
2. Which existing assets or integrations would reduce time-to-value?
3. What production, security, data-rights, and reliability evidence is required?
4. Would a paid pilot, commercial integration, investment, or acquisition be the right path?
5. What measurable result would justify expanding the relationship?

## Contact and next step

Request a product-fit discussion through the project owner. Before any transaction, both parties should review software licensing, ownership/assignment records, data-source terms, hosting dependencies, security posture, and the exact assets included.
